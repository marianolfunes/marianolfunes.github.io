Milestones, not processes. Mariano, 6-Sep-2026. Submission is the act of delivering the offer. At award and contract signing the offer becomes a contract, and the instrument is no longer the pliego. Handover is when the works are delivered and the client signs them as accepted. Negotiation is not a step either: a public tender is submitted and decided. Whether private work runs the same way is in To confirm.

CRM is separate because the client relationship runs before, during and after every tender. Mariano, 7-Sep-2026.

Process 8 returns into 6 when a circular amends the pliego after the cost is built. Process 11 returns into 1: what a job made feeds the next bid.

What stays in the tools they have. Take-off from the drawings, and the resource curve, are in CyPres, and the change a circular makes to the cost is made in CyPres, until the core build in 6 replaces it. The dates from the obligations register go into Outlook. The target margin and the risk position are theirs.

The last column is a hypothesis to be tested in discovery. Process 1's quick wins are thin and get reworked — Mariano, 7-Sep-2026.

One project per tender

Mariano, 7-Sep-2026: this is the first quick win.

The pliego, the circulars, the offer and the notes for one tender in one shared Claude Project, worked by the Comercial team. It replaces the one shared NotebookLM licence, and everything from process 4 to 9 works out of it.

It also answers both questions the team asked on 2-Sep. One shared document per project is the Project. On tracking of comments and modifications: a comment is a question, a risk or a decision on a clause, and it becomes a dated row in the requirements matrix, the consultas list or the circular register. It can be searched, and it stays when the document it came from is superseded.

Two classes of file.

Fixed — arrives once and is not edited afterwards: the pliego as issued, the technical specifications, the drawings, the annexes, and each circular as it arrives.

Live — rewritten as the tender moves: the requirements matrix, the consultas list, the circular register, the delivery checklist, the bitácora.

Tracking goes inside the live files. Anthropic's project documentation, read 7-Sep-2026, describes no version history for project knowledge. So each live file is one table, every row dated and initialled, and a row is superseded rather than deleted.

One owner per live file. Inference: five people with edit rights re-uploading the same matrix will overwrite each other, and nothing in the product prevents it. The convention does; Claude does not enforce it.

Filenames carry meaning — circular-03.pdf, not escaneado.pdf. Anthropic, Retrieval augmented generation (RAG) for projects, read 7-Sep-2026: "Well-named files help Claude understand and retrieve the right information more effectively."

Chats are private. Anthropic, Manage project visibility and sharing, read 7-Sep-2026: chats inside a shared project are not visible to other members unless the author shares them. The group works from the files.

Structure, as a starting point:

  • Project instructions — what the tender is, who is on it, the standing rules
  • pliego-* · planos-* — fixed
  • circular-NN — fixed, one file each as they arrive
  • matriz-requisitos · consultas · registro-circulares · checklist-entrega · bitacora — live
  • The offer as it firms up — priced schedule, memoria técnica, antecedentes — live until submission, fixed after it

A template project. Same instructions, same empty live files, copied for each new tender. The second tender takes minutes to set up.

What a tender folder looks like on their disc today decides the structure.

To confirm

  • Does CyPres export, and in what format? Its site, read 4-Sep-2026, names none. Settled by asking Marcelo Gota.
  • Is the common disc SharePoint or a file server? Settled by asking IT, or by looking at one machine.
  • How many circulars does a tender typically draw? Mariano, 6-Sep-2026: enough to accumulate. The count decides whether the consolidated text in 8 is worth building.
  • How is a private job won — invited, negotiated, or tendered in form — and what share of the work is it now? The map above is a public-tender shape: pliego, circulares, apertura. If half the pipeline is negotiated, negotiation is a step and not an exception. Settled by asking how the last three private jobs were won.
  • Who prepares redeterminaciones, certificates and the final measurement? If it is Obras and not Comercial, processes 10 and 11 belong to a later wave.
  • Does Costos y Reporting already produce bid against actual, per job? It decides whether the Core entry in process 11 is a report they have or a build they need.
  • Is there an antecedentes folder — one place holding the track record, financial figures, certificates and registrations that every bid asks for? The disqualifier check in 4 and the antecedentes table in 7 both reuse it.
  • Does a precalificación stage apply to the work they bid? If it does, it is a submission of its own, ahead of the offer, and it is not on the map.
  • Is the UT agreement a step of its own? Between 5 and 7, where the decision is with a partner: scope split, shares, guarantees, signed with Legales. It has a document and a deadline, and it is not on the map.
  • Is the contract review before signing a step of its own? The award milestone currently covers it. It is the first quick win in process 10.
